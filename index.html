The State of US University AI Compute

Who owns AI compute, who can access it, and how it maps to institutional wealth and research spending. Sample caveat: these 50 schools are currently those with the most documented GPU inventory in the GPUs Per Student dataset — not an NSF-R&D-defined "top 50 research universities." Migration to an R&D-defined universe and an independently source-verified inventory is underway (see Methodology & the cluster-level audit).

Key findings

Not one audited university's legacy GPU figure held up as-is. The independent audit found substantial, non-uniform errors: duplicate accelerators (Illinois, UT Austin), cloud counted as owned (CMU), planned hardware counted as operational (Oregon State), MIG partitions as physical GPUs (Princeton), and wrong generation records (Harvard, NYU). These errors move rankings. But they aren't all overstatements — Harvard's legacy row actually under-counted its current cluster.
Ownership ≠ access ≠ funding — and it's material. Of ~7,400 verified pilot GPUs, ~28% are nationally-allocated NSF facilities (Illinois NCSA, UT Austin TACC), not institutional hardware; CMU's largest compute is reserved cloud. A single "owned GPUs" number conflates categories that behave very differently.
For several universities, public documentation can't establish a total. Stanford (Sherlock), MIT (Engaging per-model), Harvard (Cannon), JHU (DSAI) publish aggregate or no counts. Their figures are floors (Stanford ≥248), not reliable totals — a smaller discrepancy there means incomplete coverage, not a confirmed overcount.
Funding is more often federal or unknown than demonstrably institutional. Of 11 pilot schools, 6 have unverified (Unknown) funding; where documented it is frequently federal (NSF facilities) or donated (UF/NVIDIA). The dataset does not claim most schools self-fund.
Retracted since round one. "Wealthiest own the least compute per dollar" is not established — an artifact of legacy overstatement + incomplete coverage of wealthy privates. "Legacy consistently overstates" is retracted (Harvard undercounted). Broad wealth/research/compute claims await a verified sample.
