University AI Compute

Mapping the computing infrastructure of America's leading research universities — and how it relates to institutional wealth, research spending, and access.

Key findings

Three exploratory relationships across the 50 highest-R&D US universities (NSF HERD FY2024), recomputed live from the dataset. They describe documented GPU capacity (primary-source lower bounds), not complete inventories.

Compute vs. university resources

The principal view: documented GPU capacity against research spending and endowment. The biggest budgets and endowments do not track the biggest documented GPU fleets: several mid-ranked universities (Illinois, MIT, NYU) document far larger fleets than higher-spending or wealthier peers.

Who pays for & controls academic compute?

The GPUs associated with a university are mostly university-owned — but a large share is faculty-priority-allocated, not centrally controlled. The pool a general researcher can get a guaranteed block from is often a minority of the headline count: NYU 232 of 944 Torch GPUs are the general pool; UCSF 88 of 235 are communal; Columbia's general tier has none; Illinois's 1,650 are ~92% NSF national facility; Cornell's 755 span 70 owners. Idle priority-GPUs are widely shared via preemptible queues (↻).

Methodology, sources & limitations

Sample: top 50 US universities by NSF HERD FY2024 R&D expenditure — defined independently of GPU holdings. GPU capacity is collected from primary sources (university research-computing docs, NSF/DOE facility pages, official announcements) and reported as documented lower bounds; missing inventories are marked unresolved, never set to zero. Ownership ≠ access: legal ownership, funding, operation, and scheduling control are tracked separately. "H100 price-equivalent" (used only in the generation chart) is a value proxy (est. value ÷ $35k), not a performance measure. Financial comparisons are exploratory and do not establish causation or under-investment. R&D totals are not uniformly comparable: NSF HERD figures include federally funded R&D centers that do not reflect campus academic-computing demand — most notably Johns Hopkins, whose ~$4.1B includes roughly $2.6B from its Applied Physics Laboratory, so its apparent R&D-to-GPU gap is overstated.

One authoritative pipeline. Every ranking, financial comparison, summary figure, table, and the generation chart draws on a single source: the HERD-50 documented lower bounds (herd50.csv) plus independently documented cluster records (clusters.csv). A given university therefore reports the same documented total everywhere (e.g. MIT ≥1,448, NYU ≥1,276, Harvard ≥1,144, Illinois ≥1,650, Cornell ≥755). The legacy GPUs-Per-Student-derived estimates are kept only for historical comparison and data discovery and are never combined with these counts. In the generation chart, GPU generations are assigned only where model-level cluster evidence exists (8 of the 41 documented schools); the remaining documented capacity is aggregate-only and shown as "Generation unknown," never omitted and never given an invented breakdown. Of the 41 documented schools, 4 are single-source verified, ~4 more have model-level inventories, and the rest are lower bounds or aggregate-only (see each row's count type in the explorer).

Reference schools. A few notable endowment-heavy universities fall outside the HERD top-50 because their total R&D is below the rank-50 cutoff (e.g. Princeton ≥612 documented GPUs, $36B endowment; Rice ≥160, $8.5B). They are shown on the scatter by default as clearly-marked reference points (toggle "HERD-50 only" to hide them) but are excluded from every correlation and aggregate statistic, which use the HERD-50 sample only. Notre Dame and the University of Chicago are similarly endowment-heavy outsiders but publish no GPU count, so they are listed as unresolved rather than plotted (reference_points.csv).

Data-quality audit. A 10-university pilot was verified against primary sources, surfacing substantial, non-uniform errors in legacy third-party estimates (duplicate accelerators, cloud counted as owned, planned/decommissioned hardware counted as current, MIG partitions as physical GPUs). Corrections are reflected throughout; for several universities public documentation cannot establish a complete total, so figures are floors.

Ownership ≠ access ≠ funding. A substantial share of "university" GPUs are NSF/state national facilities (Illinois NCSA, UT Austin TACC, UCSD SDSC) or reserved cloud (CMU) — not institution-controlled hardware.
Documented ≠ complete. Condo and aggregate clusters often publish no per-model count; those figures are lower bounds, and 9 of 50 schools are unresolved.
Funding is often unknown. Where undocumented, funding is marked "unknown," never inferred as institutional.
Correlations are exploratory. Pearson correlations on documented lower bounds with partial coverage; they describe association, not causation, and are sensitive to the unresolved schools.
