The State of US University AI Compute

Who owns AI compute, who can access it, and how it maps to institutional wealth and research spending. Sample caveat: these 50 schools are currently those with the most documented GPU inventory in the GPUs Per Student dataset — not an NSF-R&D-defined "top 50 research universities." Migration to an R&D-defined universe and an independently source-verified inventory is underway (see Methodology & the cluster-level audit).

Key findings

Descriptive statements the dataset directly shows. Figures are based on documented owned GPUs (largely legacy GPUs-Per-Student data, now being source-verified); treat broader explanations as hypotheses, not established cause.

—
Documented owned compute does not scale with endowment. Across schools with documented inventories, the spread in owned GPUs per $B of endowment is very wide, and several of the wealthiest sit near the bottom. Caveat: this reflects documented owned hardware only — it may also reflect incomplete inventories and heavier reliance on cloud or national facilities, not lower investment alone.
Access ≠ ownership. USC, Michigan and Notre Dame each hold ~1–1.5M documented national supercomputing node-hours (DOE INCITE/ALCC) while owning few GPUs; GPU-rich NYU and Texas A&M hold little measured allocation. Allocation coverage is incomplete (INCITE/ALCC + NAIRR only; not NSF ACCESS or cloud), so absence is not proof of no access.
Where funding is documented, donations and federal facilities built the biggest fleets. Florida's traces to an NVIDIA gift; Illinois's and UT Austin's are NSF national facilities. For most schools the funding source is not yet verified and is marked Unknown — this dataset does not claim they self-funded.
Documented owned GPU does not track total R&D. Johns Hopkins — #1 in US R&D — shows little documented owned GPU here, while some smaller-R&D schools rank high. The data shows the divergence; it does not establish the cause (research mix, cloud/national reliance, or incomplete inventory are all possible).
Fleet vintage varies widely. Florida skipped the Hopper generation (Ampere → Blackwell); NYU and others carry large legacy fleets (V100/RTX8000) that loom large by GPU count but contribute little effective compute. Composition, not just count, matters.
